# DP3-01 notes: contact payments in `TxDraft`

Decisions taken where DASHPAY §2.3, the ROADMAP row, m1-engine §2.7.1 and the m4 contract are silent. The contracts
carry the outcome (m1 §2.7 and §2.7.1, m1-swift `SendViewModel`, m4 §2.5, §6 and item 48); this file says why.

## The flow

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | m4 §6's `Recipient::Contact{identity, contact, amount, subtract_fee, note}` is `TxDraft::set_contact_recipient(ContactRecipient{…})`, the same fields. It makes the contact the draft's only recipient; `set_recipients` replaces it again. Errors carry recipient index 0. | `Recipient` is the address line of every M1 host, dw-ffi's included (frozen until E0-13). A contact payment pays one contact (iOS and Android have no multi-recipient contact send), and the reservation, lock and fence below are per contact. An enum would change every M1 call site and binding for no behaviour. |
| D2 | `set_contact_recipient` validates offline from the in-memory read models: both ids Base58 (`invalid_argument`), not the identity itself (`contact.self`), an identity of the wallet (`identity.not_found`), the relation `Contact`, or `PendingIncoming` for "Accept and pay" (else `invalid_argument` "not a contact of this identity"), the channel not `Broken` (`contact.channel_broken`), no payment lock (`contact.payment_locked{txid}`), and the amount as m1 (`send.invalid_amount`, `send.dust_amount` at 546). While a writer holds the contacts state with no snapshot, `platform.unavailable`. | DASHPAY §2.3 step 1. The m1 sync-read rule: no network, no SQLite. `contact()` answers from the same model. |
| D3 | `estimate` plans the payment with a placeholder P2PKH output (an all-zero key hash), the 25-byte script every DIP-15 address has, and counts it as paid outside the wallet. | DASHPAY §2.3 step 2: the fee is identical. The real address is in the contact's external account, which `wallet_owns` would count as the wallet's; the payment always leaves the wallet, so the spend cap counts it. |
| D4 | `prepare(grant)` of a contact payment, in this order: the contact checks of D2 again, now requiring the relation `Contact` (a pending incoming request is `contact.ineligible{PendingIncoming}`: accept first); the grant's vault precheck (`send.vault_locked`); the contact crypto (D6); the plan; the lease (D5), which redeems a plain grant; the Spend charge of `amount + fee` (`send.grant_exceeded`); **then** the reservation (D7); the swap into the same-size output; signing and the input reservation. Every refusal before the reservation consumes no address. | DASHPAY §2.3 step 5 and the ROADMAP acceptance. The contact is checked before anything is redeemed, so "Accept and pay" never spends before its accept. |
| D5 | A contact payment is always leased. A lease id (`AcceptAndPay`, or the new `FlowKind::ContactPayment`) is used as given; a plain `Spend` grant becomes a one-payment `ContactPayment` lease, owned by the `PreparedTx`. `ContactPayment` carries only a `Spend` grant. | E0-04 §2a.1 and §4.6: the engine is the dispatch fence for contact payments, so Lock before the hand-off is `send.cancelled`, a definitely-unsent attempt leaves the tombstone `resolve_payment_lock` reads as `NotSent`, and the Spend charge is bound to the txid. M1 address sends stay `Unleased(Send)` until P5. |
| D6 | The reservation drains contact crypto through the lease's own (an `AcceptAndPay` lease's `PlatformOp`), else the wallet's background `DashPayCrypto` (vault unlocked or unencrypted), else none: a provider that refuses every call. The library's drain returns before touching a provider when nothing is queued, the normal case once bring-up or the last unlock drained. With crypto queued and no provider, `prepare` is `send.vault_locked` before the grant is redeemed. | A `Spend` grant cannot yield DashPay crypto: dw-vault issues Platform scopes only from `PlatformOp` grants, and widening that is out of scope (least privilege, DASHPAY §2.6). The refusal names what helps: unlock. |
| D7 | The reserved address is kept per (wallet, identity, contact) for the session (D21) and reused by every later `prepare` to that contact until a broadcast hands the payment off (accepted or unknown). It is kept after `send.no_peers`, `send.broadcast_rejected`, `send.cancelled`, `abandon`, and any `prepare` failure after the reservation. A first broadcast takes it off the contact; a second `PreparedTx` that pays it is then `send.prepared_tx_spent`. | DASHPAY §2.3 step 5. An address that was never on the network can be reused; one that was handed off may be public, and DIP-15 rotates per payment. A re-prepare after a lock resolved `NotSent` consumes one more index, a gap of one. |
| D8 | An unknown outcome (`send.broadcast_unknown`, first or repeat) sets `dp_payment_lock(wallet, identity, contact, txid, since)`: the in-memory lock at once, then the row. A failed write is logged; the lock holds for the session. Locks load with the wallet's identity choices (session open, bring-up, wallet add). `set_contact_recipient`, `estimate`, `prepare` and a first `broadcast` refuse a locked pair (`contact.payment_locked{txid}`); a repeat broadcast of the locking transaction proceeds, and its acceptance clears the lock. `Platform{Payments}` and `Platform{Contacts}` are signalled; `ContactDetail.payment_lock` shows it. | DASHPAY §2.3 step 8, m1 §2.7.1 "Broadcast". The sync `payment_lock` read needs memory (m4 rule 3). The first broadcast's check stops a second draft prepared before the lock from sending past it. |
| D9 | `resolve_payment_lock`: `Sent` when `dispatch_status(txid)` is `Sent`, or the wallet holds the transaction in a block or InstantSend-locked; `NotSent` when `dispatch_status` is `NotSent`, or a ChainLocked wallet transaction with another txid spends one of its inputs (the inputs from the wallet's record of it, else the lock's own: D20); otherwise `Unknown`. `Sent` and `NotSent` delete the lock. | m4 §2.5 and item 34: positive evidence only. A mempool sighting is not evidence (BL-76): the wallet records its own broadcast before any peer took it. |
| D10 | `note` is stored with the transaction as its message, as an address recipient's `message` is. A contact payment writes no address-book entry. | The DIP-15 address changes with every payment; listing it would add one book row per payment to a counterparty the contacts list already names. |
| D11 | The engine writes no library `PaymentEntry`. The library's DashPay pass rebuilds sent entries from the history (`reconcile_sent_payments_from_tx_history`), and DP3-02 attributes the payment by the external pool, where the reservation marked the address used. | DASHPAY §2.3 step 7. A second writer of the entries would race the pass. |
| D12 | `subtract_fee` and every coin source apply: `Any`, `Outpoints` (coin control) and `FullyMixedOnly` (CoinJoin; no change, the rest is fee). The cap stays `amount + fee`; subtract-fee only lowers the outflow. | DASHPAY §2.3 keeps the M1 rules and "CoinJoin source allowed". |
| D13 | No per-contact mutex over `prepare`. The wallet's coin fence already serializes prepares (and so reservations) of one wallet; two drafts to one contact share the reserved address (D21), and only one first broadcast to a contact runs at a time (D20). | The lock that matters is the outcome lock; serializing whole sheets would hold one draft's sheet on another's. |
| D20 | One first broadcast per contact at a time (review r1 M1). `broadcast` claims the contact's reserved address and marks the payment under way, under one mutex with the lock read; a concurrent first broadcast to the contact, `prepare`, `estimate` and `set_contact_recipient` are `contact.payment_locked{txid}` with the payment under way. The mark drops after the outcome settled the lock. A lock is never replaced by another payment's (the oldest stands); a repeat broadcast's unknown outcome writes the same row again, so a first write that failed is retried. The lock row stores the payment's inputs (`dp_payment_lock.inputs`, migration `V2026101401`), so the ChainLocked-conflict evidence of D9 survives a restart that lost the wallet's record; `load_payment_locks` merges rows into memory, where a lock whose row write failed stays. | Without the mark, two drafts' first broadcasts could both end unknown and the second's lock replace the first's: the second resolving `Sent` would unlock the contact while the first may still confirm, a double payment. A lock no evidence can clear after a restart would block the contact for good. `payment_lock` stays the persisted lock only; the mark lasts about a minute. |
| D21 | The reserved address lives in the session per (wallet, identity, contact), not on the draft: an abandoned draft's address is the next draft's. It is not persisted: a restart before any broadcast costs one index per contact, and so does a first broadcast that ends never-sent after another `prepare` reserved anew meanwhile (the first address stays out of the pool). | Every abandoned draft costing an index could walk a contact past its DIP-15 gap limit, so its wallet would miss later payments. Persisting it would need its own row and invalidation for a gap of one per restart. |
| D22 | Review r1, kept or deferred: a fence `Deferred` on a first broadcast stays `send.broadcast_unknown` with a lock (the fence documents it as MaybeSent: another copy may be out). The one-payment `ContactPayment` lease ends with its last owner, the `PreparedTx`, as a host-held lease does. Holding the coin fence across `begin_lease` and the reservation cannot deadlock: a vault lock's gate takes no coin fence and its drain waits only on lease scopes. `prepare` reads the relation live (a sync's write lock waits instead of a stale snapshot refusing "Accept and pay"). Crypto queued after D6's check makes the drain's refusal (`platform.signer_unavailable`) `send.vault_locked`. D6's check counts every queued entry of the wallet, also other contacts': with a vault that prompts per send, unlock once. A broadcast that settles after its wallet was removed leaves the address and lock alone (review r2 #3: a re-added wallet must not inherit them). A bring-up reload racing a `clear_payment_lock` may restore that lock for the session; `resolve_payment_lock` clears it again from the same evidence (review r2 #2). | Each would add mechanism for a case the existing rule already handles safely, or is the wallet-wide rule the library's drain itself applies. |
| D19 | A contact whose channel is `SetupPending` is payable. The reservation's drain builds a queued paying account through the payment's crypto (D6); when the account is missing with nothing queued (the library's sweep has not built it yet), the reservation fails after the grant was redeemed, as `platform.*`, with no address taken. | Only `Broken` is unpayable (DASHPAY §2.3). Refusing every `SetupPending` contact would block the common case where the drain is what finishes the setup; the rare miss costs one grant and no address. |
| D23 | **The lock row is written before the first hand-off** (Sol r1 Blocker). A contact payment's first `broadcast` claims the contact (D20), then writes its `dp_payment_lock` row (pair, txid, inputs) and awaits the write, before the dispatch fence can hand the bytes to a peer. A failed write refuses the hand-off: the broadcast returns the store's error (`storage`), the payment is `Pending` again and its address back with the contact. The row is the record of every payment that may have gone out; memory gets the lock only from an unknown outcome (or a load), so `payment_lock` stays `None` while the first broadcast runs in this process. `set_dp_payment_lock` never replaces another payment's row: it returns that row's txid, and the broadcast is `contact.payment_locked{txid}`, so app.sqlite decides at the hand-off even if memory lost a lock. Definitely unsent (`Pending` or `Released` after a first broadcast) drops the row; accepted drops it; unknown keeps it. `load_payment_locks` merges the rows into memory and settles each loaded lock with D9's positive evidence (the fence's `Sent`/`NotSent`, a block or InstantSend sighting, a ChainLocked conflict), skipping a pair whose first broadcast runs here; anything else stays locked. `PaymentLocks::forget` (unload, removal) drops memory only: the next load reads every row back. A settlement skipped because the wallet was unloaded meanwhile leaves its row for that load. No new migration: the row and its `inputs` column (V2026101401) already hold the intent; main took no later version. | A crash or unload between the hand-off and the old post-outcome write lost the only record of a possibly sent payment, and a second payment to the contact could start. The dispatch journal names the artifact, not the contact pair. |
| D24 | **One attempt at a time per prepared payment** (Sol r1 Major). `PreparedTx` holds an async attempt mutex across `broadcast_inner`, through the contact settlement; the phase leaves `Broadcasting` only after `settle_contact`. A repeat started while the first attempt settles waits, then runs as a repeat. All lock changes (memory and row together: `record_payment`, `lock_payment`, `clear_payment_lock`, `load_payment_locks`) run under one session-wide async `rows` mutex held across their app.sqlite job, so a clear's delete never lands before an earlier insert, and a load never interleaves with them. | A repeat could be accepted and clear before the first attempt locked, leaving an accepted payment locked; a blocking-pool insert and delete could also land in reverse order. |
| D25 | **dwcli "Accept and pay" partial success** (Sol r1 Minor). Once `accept_request` went through, a failed `prepare` or `broadcast` is `{"ok":true,"result":{"grant","accepted":true,"paid":false,"payment_error":{code,message,params},"next"}}`, not an error: `next` says to pay again without `--accept` (a new `Spend` grant), or, for `send.broadcast_unknown` or a panic, that the contact is locked until `contact resolve-lock`. A full success adds `"accepted": true` to the payment's result. | D15's "Accepted — payment cancelled. Pay now?"; `--accept` again would be refused (already a contact). |
| D26 | `ContactRecipient`'s DTO carries its field docs on the struct: ts-rs writes a field doc comment after a trailing space, which `git diff --check` flags (Sol r1 Nit). The generator is left alone; changing it rewrites every generated file. | Narrow fix. |

## "Accept and pay"

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D14 | No new facade call. The host: `set_contact_recipient` on the pending contact, `estimate`, the sheet, one `authorize_set` (a `PlatformOp` capped by `grant_request(AcceptRequest)` and a `Spend` of `amount + fee`), one `begin_flow(AcceptAndPay)`, `accept_request(lease)`, then `prepare(lease)` and `broadcast`. `prepare` refuses until the accept established the contact (D4), so nothing is spent before it. `dwcli pay-contact --accept` runs exactly this with one credential. | m4 §1 "Grants and leases", E0-04 §16.1. The parts exist since DP2-02 and E0-04; one more call would duplicate the accept's refusals. |
| D15 | After an accept that succeeded and a payment that did not (`send.cancelled`, `platform.lease_revoked{Lock}`), the outcome is E0-04's C8 ("Accepted — payment cancelled. Pay now?"): the contact is established, the address stays reserved (D7), and a new prepare needs a new `Spend` grant. | E0-04 §4.6. |
