A desktop Dash wallet for macOS, Windows and Linux: the DashPay wallet of the iOS and Android apps, on the desktop.
One recovery phrase holds your Dash wallet, your DashPay username and contacts, your Shielded balance and your
Platform addresses. It also has the Dash Core tools a desktop user needs (coin control, PSBT, signing messages,
opening a Dash Core `wallet.dat`), and the masternode owner's screens: governance voting, registration and key
changes.

**No security review has been done. Use small amounts only.** The app says so on first run.

## What is in 1.0

- **Wallets:**
  - Create a wallet with a 12- or 24-word recovery phrase, or restore one from a 12-, 15-, 18-, 21- or 24-word
    phrase, from a backup file, or from a Dash Core `wallet.dat` (legacy Berkeley DB, encrypted or not).
  - Write down and verify the phrase. Keep several wallets, and choose which open at start.
  - When you restore, give a date or block height to start from. A restore from the first block says it will be slow.
- **Security:**
  - A passphrase lock, or the system's secret store, and auto-lock.
  - Unlock once: with "Ask for my passphrase for every payment" off (Settings ▸ Security), for five minutes after you
    unlock, payments that add up to no more than your spending limit need no passphrase. A vault created in 1.0 with
    a passphrase starts with that switch off. A vault from an earlier version that never stored a choice, or one
    created without a passphrase and encrypted later, asks for every payment until you turn the switch off.
  - The lock screen hides the balance, history and names.
  - On macOS and Windows, screens that show a recovery phrase or keys keep themselves out of screen captures. Linux
    has no such protection; don't share or record your screen while one is open.
  - Contact payments refuse when the Platform data behind them can't be verified.
- **Send and receive:**
  - Receive by address, QR code or `dash:` link. Pay to an address, a contact, a `dash:` link, a QR code read from an
    image or the screen, or a link dropped on the window.
  - InstantSend, coin control, fee choice and subtract-fee.
  - Advanced: an unsigned transaction (PSBT).
  - Warnings when a payment mixes CoinJoin and unmixed coins, or leaves a large amount to the fee.
- **Activity:**
  - History for the Dash wallet, Shielded and Platform addresses, with details for each row. A Dash wallet
    transaction shows its raw hex, and its fee when every input is the wallet's own (a payment you sent). Shielded
    and Platform rows show the fee, or an estimate marked as one, where there is one.
  - Labels and notes. Export CSV of the Dash wallet's transactions (whether confirmed, date, type, label, address,
    amount and transaction ID, plus a watch-only column when the wallet has watch-only addresses).
- **DashPay:**
  - Register a username (contested or not), set your profile and picture, and top up or withdraw your credits.
  - Contacts: search, requests, accept, pay a contact, history and notifications.
  - Claim an invitation from a link or pasted text.
- **Shielded:**
  - The Shielded balance. Its receive address is on testnet only (see below).
  - Move from the Dash wallet to Shielded and back, send to a shielded address, and move from Shielded to your own
    Platform address (testnet only; see below).
  - Max on each move. The balance says how current it is.
  - An idle wallet syncs only when the shielded pool has changed.
- **Platform addresses:**
  - The balance, activity, and "Move to Dash wallet" (test networks; see below).
  - Transfers between the Dash wallet, Shielded and Platform (Shielded on testnet only), and finishing an unfinished transfer.
  - Recover what is left of a transfer to Shielded that Platform refused after charging a penalty: choose Recover
    under Unfinished transfers on Home to move it to your Platform balance, less Platform's fee (test networks).
- **CoinJoin:**
  - Mixing with start, stop and settings, and progress with rounds.
  - Denominations are promoted and demoted as in Dash Core after V24 (test networks; see below).
  - Coins mixed in Dash Core count as mixed after you restore the same phrase.
  - CoinJoin Recovery, and an explanation of how mixing works.
- **Masternodes and governance:**
  - Shown automatically when the wallet holds masternode keys or work (Settings ▸ Show Masternodes: On, Auto or Off).
  - My masternodes: masternodes whose keys are in this wallet and ones you track, with status and payouts, and an
    evonode's Platform node details. Showing and withdrawing an evonode's claimable credits come later.
  - Masternode keys: each secret is behind the passphrase.
  - Add a tracked masternode's voting key and vote with it.
  - Governance: proposals, voting with one or many masternodes, and creating a proposal.
  - Username voting on contested names.
  - Registering a masternode or evonode, and changing its keys or payout (test networks; see below).
  - If a masternode transaction never confirms, abandoning it in Activity releases the coins it reserved.
  - Shared masternodes (v24): start, join, approve, sign and send a shared registration, and once it's registered,
    dissolve it alone or together with the other owners, create, save and send its standby dissolution, and agree a
    change of its keys with the other owners (test networks; see below). This version doesn't send a key change: once
    everyone has signed, you can keep the session for a later version or discard it. Changing a share's reward address
    isn't in the app yet. Discard and abandon, the ways out, are.
- **Tools:**
  - Sign and verify messages, and the console.
  - Information and Sync progress, with each phase.
  - Repair: rescan, reset chain data, and remove unconfirmed transactions.
- **Settings:**
  - The network (mainnet or testnet), local currency, the minimum decimal places shown, and language.
  - Backups: automatic, Choose File, and Show Folder.
  - About: version, license, source code, where to report a problem, and updates.
- **Updates:** Settings ▸ About ▸ Updates checks for a new version, by hand or automatically (off by default). An
  update installs only when you choose to, and only after its signature is checked.
- **Language:** English.
- **Accessibility:** keyboard-only use of the DashPay journeys, and screen-reader names checked on Linux.

### Off on mainnet in 1.0

These work on testnet (and on regtest and devnet), and the app doesn't offer them on mainnet until each has had its
own funds review. On mainnet the app says they are available on test networks only, or doesn't show them.

- Registering a masternode or evonode, and changing its keys or payout (ProRegTx and ProUpRegTx sends).
- Shared masternodes: starting, joining, approving, signing and sending a shared registration, approving a key change,
  and sending a dissolution, a standby line or a key change. A shared masternode you're part of is still listed, and
  its way-out steps (discard, abandon) still run. You can still approve a dissolution together and save the standby
  file, and the app explains how to leave by sending the standby line from Dash Core's console.
- Paying a contact from the Platform balance.
- Topping up a username's credits from the Platform balance.
- Paying for a username from your Platform addresses (under Advanced when you register).
- Shielded: the receive address, moving into Shielded, paying for a username from it, and sending or moving from it
  (to the Dash wallet, to a shielded address or to your own Platform address). Unlike the rest of this list, these
  work on testnet only, not on regtest or devnet. Elsewhere, where Shielded is active, the app says this version can't
  send from Shielded yet, and payments to a Shielded address you gave out earlier still show in your Shielded balance.
- Paying several recipients in one payment.
- Moving from a Platform address to your Dash wallet ("Move to Dash wallet"), and sending from Platform to an address.
  Mainnet Platform already has address funds, but the app keeps these off on mainnet until a later release, after
  a mainnet trial. On a test network whose Platform doesn't have address funds active yet, the app shows "Platform
  addresses are not active on this network yet".
- CoinJoin promoting and demoting denominations (V24 conversions). Mixing itself works on mainnet.

Paying a contact from the Shielded balance is not offered on any network in 1.0.

Shared-masternode sends stay off on mainnet until two recovery fixes land: a safe retry after an interrupted Abandon,
and a Send again after a database restore that can't hand off a costlier transaction than it charges.

## Fixes

Fixed since 0.3:

- **Platform address sends:** a send that the network refused could be shown as failed even though it might still go
  through, so sending again could pay the recipient twice. Dash Wallet now keeps such a send pending until it is
  settled.

## Known limitations

- **No security review.** Do not keep more than you can afford to lose.
- **Release candidates are not notarized on macOS and not code-signed on Windows.** The first launch shows a
  warning; see Install. `SHA256SUMS` and every file are attested by GitHub's build provenance.
- **English only.**
- **Mixing (CoinJoin) is experimental.** A normal payment or a move to Shielded doesn't use coins that are mixing or
  mixed. To spend fully mixed coins, open Pay ▸ "Advanced: coins, fee and change" and choose "Fully mixed funds only
  (CoinJoin)". While mixing, the total on the CoinJoin page can move as sessions hold coins; Home's balance is right.
- **Shielded** depends on the network's Platform protocol version. Where the shielded pool isn't active, the app
  shows "Shielded is not active on this network yet". Home's history lists only Dash wallet rows; Shielded and
  Platform payments are in Activity.
- **Paying for a username on two devices (testnet):** On testnet, if two devices with the same recovery phrase each
  pay for a username from Shielded at the same time, only one of them registers it. The other device can then show the
  new identity but not be able to use it or its credits, or it can say the registration didn't go through and offer to
  pay again. Don't pay again: Dash Platform refuses it and keeps a fee. Use the identity on the device that registered
  it. If the other device said the registration didn't go through, wait until the username shows on the first device,
  then open Dash Wallet again on the other one.
- **Payments that never confirm:** if a payment can never confirm and the wallet can't prove which coins its
  replacement spent, Home shows those coins as held by an unfinished payment, and they stay out of the balance. Choose
  What can I do? beside that line. For most wallets, removing the wallet and restoring it from its recovery phrase
  brings back any that are unspent. Before you remove it, make sure you have its recovery phrase, its BIP39 passphrase
  if it has one (the words alone restore a different wallet), and your own copies of any masternode keys or
  shared-masternode standbys you added: the phrase doesn't restore them. A wallet added from a seed without a recovery
  phrase, or from a Dash Core wallet.dat that uses older addresses, can't be restored that way, so keep it.
- **Large wallets:** a wallet with tens of thousands of transactions takes several seconds to open, its history
  pages load slowly, and the app uses more memory than planned (over 400 MB in all).
- **Crashes while sending:** if the app quits while a payment is being sent, the payment stays pending and keeps its
  coins until it is mined or abandoned. A payment to a contact that the app hadn't handed to the network yet may not
  be listed at all after the restart, with its coins back in the balance and the contact still waiting. In either
  case, check whether it went through (for example in a block explorer) before you send it again.
- **An abandoned payment can still go through:** A payment that reached the network before you abandoned it may
  still confirm. Check with the recipient before you send the same payment again. If both confirm, you pay twice.
- **Spent coins shown again after abandoning a payment:** If you abandon a payment, or Dash Wallet drops one, while
  another wallet in Dash Wallet is still catching up (for example right after a restore), a coin that payment spent
  can show as available again even though it was already spent. A payment that uses that coin never confirms, and it
  can stay pending in Activity. Once every wallet has finished catching up, run Tools ▸ Repair ▸ Rescan from the
  wallet's birth to correct the coin list.
- **Held amount that includes a spent coin:** While a wallet catches up, the amount Home shows as held by an
  unfinished payment can include a coin that another payment already spent in a confirmed block. That coin is gone:
  removing and restoring the wallet doesn't bring it back, and nothing else is lost. Only the held amount is too
  high.
- **Restoring a wallet created in the iOS or Android app:** checked by comparing the derivation code; not yet tested
  end to end with a funded mobile wallet.
- **A coin shown as available after you abandon a payment:** Rarely, after you abandon an unconfirmed payment and
  restart Dash Wallet, a coin it used can show in your balance and in coin control although a payment you made with
  the same recovery phrase, for example on another device, already spends it. A payment from that coin can't go
  through. It goes away once that other payment is in a block.
- **Older payments missing from a wallet:** If a wallet's history starts before the block data Dash Wallet keeps, for
  example when you restore an older wallet after another one has synced, Home says its older payments aren't shown
  yet. They stay missing until you choose Reset chain data and resync, from that notice or in Tools ▸ Repair. Rarely,
  a payment can also stay missing after you set an earlier birth height, restore a recovery phrase for a wallet you
  already watch, or after a block reorganization. If you know a payment is missing, go to Tools ▸ Repair, choose From
  height under Rescan, enter a height from before that payment, then choose Rescan.
- **Balance after Dash Wallet couldn't read its saved data:** Rarely, if Dash Wallet can't read all of its saved
  payment records when it starts, your balance can count, and coin control can offer, a coin that an unconfirmed
  payment already spent. A payment from that coin can't go through. The next start that reads everything corrects
  this, so quit Dash Wallet and open it again. If saving fails right after you abandon a payment, that payment can
  show as pending again later, with its coins out of your balance; abandon it again.
- **Statuses recorded by an earlier version:** After the upgrade, an identity funding or a DashPay payment that an
  earlier version recorded as locked or confirmed can keep that status although the network never confirmed it. The
  identity can then show as ready to create, and Dash Platform can refuse it. A DashPay payment can show as confirmed
  while it's still unconfirmed. Before you rely on such a payment, check in a block explorer that it's in a block.
- **A dropped payment counted again after a restart:** After a restart, if the network still relays a payment you
  abandoned, or one that lost to another payment, your balance can include money from it that won't arrive, although
  Activity still shows it as Abandoned. You can't abandon it again. Your balance is right again once that payment, or
  another payment that spends the same coins, is in a block.
- **A pending payment you didn't make:** Rarely, a transaction that claims to spend one of your coins without your
  signature can show in Activity as a pending payment from this wallet, and change it claims to pay you can count in
  your balance as unconfirmed. It can never confirm. Dash Wallet removes most of these by itself once it has synced.
  If one stays and you don't use this recovery phrase on another device, open it in Activity and choose Abandon on
  that payment itself, not on a later payment that spends from it, and don't use Remove unconfirmed transactions for
  it. Then choose Report a problem.
- **Abandoning a payment to another of your wallets:** Dash Wallet won't abandon or remove an unconfirmed payment once
  a later locked or confirmed payment spends from it, but it sees later payments only in wallets that are open. If you
  paid another of your wallets in Dash Wallet, open that wallet and let it finish opening before you use Abandon or
  Remove unconfirmed transactions. Otherwise the payment's coins can show as available again, and a new payment from
  them fails, until the original payment confirms.
- **A Platform send that never goes through (test networks):** If Dash Platform never carries out a send from your
  Platform addresses that you approved, Dash Wallet keeps it pending, and new sends, top-ups and contact payments from
  your Platform balance are refused until it settles. To use your Platform balance again, open that send in Activity
  and choose Move to Dash wallet: only one of the two can go through, and if the move does, the recipient isn't paid.
  A held move to your Dash wallet or a held contact payment has Cancel instead. Sending from your Dash wallet isn't
  affected.
- **Restoring earlier data after the update:** Restore earlier data doesn't know about payments, or Dash Platform
  username or identity registrations and credit top-ups, that you made after the update and that haven't confirmed
  yet. Before you restore, note any such payment. Afterwards, its funds can show as available, and a new payment from
  them can fail or make the earlier payment fail. Dash Wallet may also be unable to finish a registration or top-up
  you started then. Don't send that payment again until it confirms or the recipient tells you it didn't arrive.
- **Repairing data from an earlier version:** If Dash Wallet can't finish repairing wallet data that an earlier
  version wrote, it tries again each time it opens that wallet, so quit Dash Wallet and open it again. Until the
  repair succeeds, a coin you already spent can show as available. Don't spend it, and if this keeps happening, choose
  Report a problem.
- **Dash Wallet keeps offering only Try again when it starts:** If a network's wallet data is damaged, Dash Wallet can
  offer only Try again when it opens that network. To use your wallets again, quit Dash Wallet and move that network's
  folder (for example mainnet or testnet) out of Dash Wallet's data folder. Don't delete it. The data folder is
  ~/Library/Application Support/org.dash.wallet.desktop on macOS, %APPDATA%\org.dash.wallet.desktop on Windows,
  ~/.local/share/org.dash.wallet.desktop on Linux, and ~/.var/app/org.dash.wallet.desktop/data/org.dash.wallet.desktop
  for the Flatpak. Then open Dash Wallet and set up each wallet again from its recovery phrase, with its BIP39
  passphrase if it has one. Keep the folder you moved: it still holds any masternode keys or shared-masternode
  standbys you added, which the recovery phrase doesn't restore. Also see “Setting up from a recovery phrase when your
  wallet data can't be used”.
- **A removed wallet stays in the copy from before the upgrade:** When Dash Wallet upgraded your wallet data, it kept
  a copy of the data from before the upgrade, in the upgrade-backups folder inside the network's folder (for example
  mainnet). Removing a wallet doesn't delete it from that copy, so the wallet's history, addresses and labels stay on
  this computer, and Restore earlier data can bring the wallet back into the list without its keys. To remove it from
  this computer completely, quit Dash Wallet after you remove the wallet, then delete the upgrade-backups folder
  inside that network's folder. You then can't restore earlier data for any wallet on that network. Delete it only if
  all of these are true: Dash Wallet has opened that network normally since the update, with no upgrade, restore, Try
  again or recovery screen; you have never used Restore earlier data or Set up from recovery phrase on that network;
  and the folder contains no file named RESTORED, PLATFORM_PENDING, RESTORE-IN-PROGRESS or RESTORE-ATTEMPT, and no
  folder whose name starts with after-upgrade, during-restore or restore-staging. Otherwise keep it. The data folder
  is ~/Library/Application Support/org.dash.wallet.desktop on macOS, %APPDATA%\org.dash.wallet.desktop on Windows,
  ~/.local/share/org.dash.wallet.desktop on Linux, and ~/.var/app/org.dash.wallet.desktop/data/org.dash.wallet.desktop
  for the Flatpak.
- **Shielded balance after updating (test networks):** If you updated from an earlier version and Dash Wallet starts
  locked, or unlocked for mixing only, a wallet that has a Dash Platform identity, such as a DashPay username, may not
  update its Shielded balance or activity, and Shielded payments it was still sending wait, until you unlock Dash
  Wallet fully and open a Shielded screen.
- **A payment missing after a block is replaced:** Rarely, if a block is replaced shortly after it was mined, a
  payment in it can disappear from Activity, and its coins can show as available and count in your balance until the
  payment is mined again. A new payment from those coins fails. Don't send the same payment again from other coins:
  the first one can still go through, and you would pay twice. It shows in Activity again once it is mined.
- **Signing in to an app again (test networks):** Rarely, if Dash Wallet's saved records are damaged or were changed
  outside Dash Wallet, signing in to an app again can disable a key of your Dash Platform identity other than the one
  that app was given.
- **Regtest sync:** On regtest, Sync progress can stop at Block filters, so Dash Wallet doesn't catch up with the
  network and new payments don't show. Testnet and mainnet aren't affected.
- **Masternode holds released too early (testnet):** On testnet, if another transaction conflicts with a masternode
  registration or change you made, Dash Wallet can stop holding what it set aside for it too early, even though the
  registration or change can still confirm. Its collateral or fee coins can show as available, withdrawals of the
  evonode's credits can stop waiting for a payout-address change, and you can make another change to the masternode
  before the earlier one is settled. This can happen when the conflicting transaction's block is later replaced, isn't
  final yet, or competes with an InstantSend-locked registration or change. If you spend those coins in the meantime,
  the registration or change can fail, or a registration that confirms first loses its masternode again. Platform can
  pay a withdrawal to the new payout address from your change instead of the one Dash Wallet showed when you approved
  it, and the masternode can end up with the earlier change's keys or payout address.
- **Balance lower after a restart:** After a restart, your Dash wallet balance can show lower than it should. If it
  looks lower than before the restart, go to Tools ▸ Repair, choose From the wallet's birth under Rescan, then choose
  Rescan.
- **Coins held back after a reorganization:** Rarely, after a chain reorganization and a restart of Dash Wallet, Home
  can keep asking you to wait for coins held back by a dropped payment, even after that payment can no longer
  complete. Those coins stay set aside, so nothing is sent from them, until an update clears the wait.
- **Held coins shown as waiting after opening:** Right after Dash Wallet opens, Home can show coins held back by a
  dropped payment as waiting, even if they were settled before. This clears once Dash Wallet is online and receives
  the next block. Until then those coins stay set aside, so nothing is sent from them.
- **Setting up from a recovery phrase when your wallet data can't be used:** If you set up Dash Wallet from your
  recovery phrase because its data was damaged or couldn't be restored, the new setup has no record of payments that
  hadn't finished in that data. Dash Wallet won't send a payment that was prepared there but not yet sent. The app
  can't tell whether such a payment had already reached the network, so if you still need to make it, check with the
  recipient that it didn't arrive before you send it again. ...before you send it again. A payment that was already
  sent can still complete. Until it does, the funds it uses can show as available, even after syncing has finished. A
  new payment from those funds can then fail, or make the earlier payment fail. The same funds are never spent twice,
  but if you send the same payment again from other funds, both can complete.
- **Paying a contact after a restart:** After each start, Dash Wallet checks your contacts' payment details on Dash
  Platform again before you can pay them. This usually takes about 2 seconds. Until it has finished, a payment to a
  contact waits and nothing is sent. If Dash Platform can't be reached, this takes longer.
- **A stuck move into Shielded from an earlier version:** If moving funds into Shielded got stuck in an earlier
  version, Resolve stuck funding in Tools ▸ Repair can stay unavailable for it in this version.
- **Shielded balance after a restart (testnet):** After you restart Dash Wallet following a Shielded payment, your
  Shielded balance can include funds that payment already spent. A payment from those funds is refused, and the
  balance is corrected when Dash Wallet syncs again.
- **Shielded payments from two devices:** If you use the same recovery phrase on another device and both send from the
  same Shielded funds, this device can show its payment as done even though the other device's payment went through
  instead. Those funds were spent by the other device's payment. On testnet, this can also happen to a Shielded
  payment from an older version that was still in progress when you upgraded. If such a payment shows as done, check
  with the recipient that it arrived.
- **Shielded payments from earlier test builds:** If you used an earlier test build, Dash Wallet may not know whether
  one of your Shielded payments went through. Before you send from Shielded, it asks you to confirm, because that
  payment may still go through. Until those Shielded funds have been sent, closing or removing that wallet or
  switching networks is refused because Dash Wallet doesn't know yet whether that payment went through.
- **Shielded payment record:** If Dash Wallet can't read its record of Shielded payments, closing the wallet,
  switching networks or restoring wallet data from before the upgrade is refused because Dash Wallet doesn't know yet
  whether a Shielded payment went through, even if none was made. Quit Dash Wallet and open it again.
- **Shielded accounts:** Funds in Shielded accounts other than the first aren't shown in Dash Wallet.
- **Abandoning a payment during a Platform transfer:** Don't abandon an unconfirmed payment while a credit top-up, a
  username registration, or a transfer to Shielded or to a Platform address is being set up from it, including in
  another of your wallets that it paid. Dash Wallet doesn't stop the abandon in that case. The transfer can still be
  sent, and a payment that later uses those coins can fail.
- **Deleting Shielded data by hand:** If the Shielded data file is deleted from Dash Wallet's data folder, Dash Wallet
  starts with empty Shielded data and doesn't warn you. It then scans for your Shielded funds again, which can take a
  while, and your Shielded balance can show as empty until it has. A Shielded payment that was still in progress when
  the file was deleted is no longer tracked: until it completes, its funds can show as available again, a new payment
  from them can fail or make that earlier payment fail, and it may not appear in your history. Don't delete files from
  the data folder.
- **A Shielded payment shown as done:** Rarely, a Shielded payment carried over from an earlier version can show as
  done even if it didn't go through. This needs Dash Wallet to fail to save its data twice, the same Shielded funds to
  be spent from another device with the same recovery phrase, and then a restart. No funds are spent or offered twice.
- **Pending coins from unconfirmed payments:** A coin from an unconfirmed payment may stay pending, and out of your
  spendable balance, if Dash Wallet can't tell whether that payment will confirm. If it never confirms, abandon the
  payment in Activity; coins it spent come back once Dash Wallet has checked that they haven't been spent elsewhere.
  If it confirms later, the coin is counted again.
- **A payment overturned after an InstantSend lock:** Very rarely, when a payment locked by InstantSend is overturned
  by a different payment confirmed with a ChainLock, the overturned payment can keep showing as pending, coins it used
  can stay out of your balance, and money it sent to you can show as available. A payment that uses that money stays
  pending and never confirms. To put this right, quit and reopen Dash Wallet first, then remove the wallet and restore
  it from its recovery phrase.
- **A payment another device beat:** If another device using the same recovery phrase spends some of the same coins as
  a payment you sent from this device, and the other device's payment confirms first, your payment can't go through.
  It disappears from Activity without a notice, and any of its coins the other payment didn't spend return to your
  balance.
- **Balance after a rescan or an abandon:** After Tools ▸ Repair ▸ Rescan, or after you abandon a payment, your
  balance can include coins that a payment made elsewhere and not yet confirmed has already spent. Elsewhere means
  another device, a CoinJoin round or a shared payment. Dash Wallet doesn't offer those coins for sending, and the
  balance corrects itself when that payment confirms or is abandoned.
- **PSBT coins aren't reserved:** Signing a PSBT doesn't reserve its coins. Until that transaction is broadcast and
  Dash Wallet sees it, Dash Wallet can use the same coins in another payment, and only one of the two will confirm. If
  you plan to broadcast the PSBT later, lock its coins in Tools ▸ Coin Selection (Lock unspent).
- **A CoinJoin round when Dash Wallet closes:** If Dash Wallet closes after signing a CoinJoin round and before it
  sees that round's transaction, the coins in that round can be offered again after the next start, for example by
  Move mixed coins. If the round's transaction went through, that payment can't confirm: abandon it in Activity. The
  coins settle when the round's transaction is mined.
- **Masternode changes on two computers:** If you use the same masternode wallet on more than one computer, one
  computer may not know about a credit withdrawal or payout-address change made on another until that change confirms.
  Make withdrawals and payout changes from one computer, or wait for a payout change to confirm before you withdraw on
  another.
- **Payout changes paid from elsewhere:** If a payout change is paid for from another wallet, from Dash Core, or from
  a wallet that isn't open on this computer, Dash Wallet doesn't see it, so it doesn't hold a withdrawal while that
  change is unconfirmed. Until the change confirms, usually within a few minutes, a withdrawal can still go to the old
  payout address.
- **Masternode payouts to a script address:** If your masternode's payout address is a script (P2SH) address, don't
  withdraw its Platform credits with the owner key yet: they would go to an address nobody can spend.
- **Quitting while a Platform node doesn't answer:** If a Platform node stops answering, quitting Dash Wallet or
  switching networks can take up to about a minute and then report that it didn't shut down cleanly. If a wallet was
  rescanning its blocks at the time, it rescans them again when you next start Dash Wallet, and you can't send from it
  until that finishes.
- **Checking contacts' payment details:** Payments to DashPay contacts now use only payment details that Dash Wallet
  has checked against the masternode list it keeps itself. A first payment to a new contact, or to one whose details
  changed, can wait a few minutes until that check is possible. Names and profiles not yet checked are marked
  Unverified. The masternode list is not yet tied to the blockchain.
- **Flatpak:** a wallet needs a passphrase (the sandbox can't reach the system secret store), and the Flatpak keeps
  its data in its own folder, so it doesn't see the wallets of an AppImage or .deb install.
- **Not in 1.0:** creating invitations, the username marketplace, DashConnect, Explore and other partner services,
  Tor, and Windows Hello and Touch ID unlock. On Linux, the tray icon shows only where the desktop has AppIndicator
  support.

## Install

- **macOS:** open the dmg and drag Dash Wallet to Applications. A release candidate isn't notarized, so macOS says
  it can't check the app for malicious software. On macOS 15 and later, open System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security
  and choose **Open Anyway** next to the Dash Wallet message, then confirm; on macOS 14 and earlier, right-click the
  app ▸ **Open**, then **Open** again. Or run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Dash Wallet.app"`.
- **Windows:** run the installer. A release candidate isn't signed: at "Windows protected your PC" choose
  **More info ▸ Run anyway**.
- **Linux:** `chmod +x dash-wallet-1.0.0-linux-x86_64.AppImage && ./dash-wallet-1.0.0-linux-x86_64.AppImage`,
  `sudo apt install ./dash-wallet-1.0.0-linux-amd64.deb`, or
  `flatpak install --user ./dash-wallet-1.0.0-linux-x86_64.flatpak` and then `flatpak run org.dash.wallet.desktop`.

For a release candidate, the file names carry its version (`1.0.0-rc.N` in place of `1.0.0`).

## Upgrading

Install 1.0 over your current version. Your wallets, settings, labels and contacts stay where they are. Keep your
recovery phrase at hand, and make a backup in Settings ▸ Backups, before you upgrade, as with any wallet upgrade.

- **From 0.1.x, 0.2.0 or 0.3.x:** install 1.0 by hand, as above. 1.0 is signed with a new update key that earlier
  versions don't know: 0.3.x may offer 1.0 in Check for updates, but the download fails its signature check and
  your current version keeps running. From 1.0 on, Settings ▸ About ▸ Check for updates offers new releases. Copies
  that keep the data in a Flatpak or a .deb update through Flatpak or apt; Settings says how.
- **The first start after an upgrade:** a wallet from an earlier version, created or restored there, that has a
  CoinJoin account or a custom address lookahead (for example one restored in Dash Core compatibility mode), and
  that hasn't had its one-time rescan yet, rescans once from its birth; you can send once it finishes. A wallet that
  already had it resumes where it was. If a restored wallet's history starts
  before the block headers Dash Wallet has stored, the app says so and asks you to run Tools ▸ Repair ▸ Reset chain
  data and resync.
- **Transfers an earlier version hid as already used:** Dash Wallet now confirms with Platform, using a proof, whether
  a transfer to a Platform address, or a restored transfer to Shielded, was already used, instead of taking Platform's
  word for it. Until that check succeeds, the transfer shows as Checking, and a transfer to a Platform address keeps
  its activity pending; this usually takes seconds once Dash Wallet can reach Platform. After this update, such
  transfers that an earlier version hid as already used can show again: choose Finish or Check again to settle them.
- **From a development build** (`0.x.y-dev.N`, from the `continuous` release or a per-commit build): development
  builds don't update themselves. Install 1.0 by hand. It opens the same data folder. If you used a development build
  on mainnet, check your balance and history after the first start, and run Tools ▸ Repair ▸ Rescan if anything looks
  wrong.
- **From a release candidate** (`1.0.0-rc.N`): install 1.0 by hand; candidates aren't offered updates.
- **From 0.1:** "Include my username in payment requests" (Settings ▸ Privacy) reads off. Turn it back on if you
  want it.
- **Going back to a version earlier than 1.0.0-rc.1 after installing this one isn't supported.** An earlier version
  may offer coins this version is holding back, and a payment that uses them can fail.
- **The earlier macOS app (the Swift one):** 1.0 doesn't read its data. To move a wallet from it, write down its
  recovery phrase in the Swift app first, and its BIP39 passphrase if it has one (the Swift app shows both). Restore
  them in 1.0 and check the balance and history there before you remove the Swift app or its data.
- **macOS:** after you install 1.0 over an earlier version, macOS may ask again for access to the Keychain, or a
  wallet kept without a passphrase may ask you to unlock it once.

## Verify

Every file above and `SHA256SUMS` are attested by GitHub's build provenance: built by this repository's release
workflow from the tagged commit. The updates the app installs are also signed with the update key built into the
app, which it checks before installing.

```sh
sha=$(gh api repos/PastaPastaPasta/dashwallet-desktop/commits/v1.0.0 --jq .sha)
gh attestation verify dash-wallet-1.0.0-linux-x86_64.AppImage --repo PastaPastaPasta/dashwallet-desktop \
  --signer-workflow PastaPastaPasta/dashwallet-desktop/.github/workflows/release.yml \
  --source-ref refs/tags/v1.0.0 --source-digest "$sha"
gh attestation verify SHA256SUMS --repo PastaPastaPasta/dashwallet-desktop \
  --signer-workflow PastaPastaPasta/dashwallet-desktop/.github/workflows/release.yml \
  --source-ref refs/tags/v1.0.0 --source-digest "$sha"
sha256sum -c --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS      # Linux; on macOS: grep <file name> SHA256SUMS | shasum -a 256 -c
```

For a release candidate, use its tag (`v1.0.0-rc.N`) and its file names. See `docs/release/verifying.md` in the
repository for the details.
